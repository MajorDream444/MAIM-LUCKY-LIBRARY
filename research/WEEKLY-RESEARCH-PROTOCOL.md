# Weekly Living World Research Protocol

## Purpose

Keep the Lucky universe connected to the real world without allowing trends to make the books disposable.

The research system has two clocks:

- **Long clock** - history, philosophy, culture, museums, institutions, enduring human behavior
- **Short clock** - the last 30 days of discussion, experimentation, concern, humor, language, technology, and youth culture

Stories become evergreen when short-clock signals are translated into long-clock human patterns.

## Recommended Weekly Cycle

### 1. Scan

Use Last 30 Days and approved research tools to examine:

- AI literacy and education
- What students, parents, and teachers are asking
- Youth culture and language
- Media literacy and misinformation
- Sports, confidence, pressure, and identity
- Museums, exhibitions, discoveries, and historical anniversaries
- Global citizenship and intercultural learning
- Child safety, privacy, accessibility, and inclusion
- Entrepreneurship and future-of-work signals
- Climate, food, health, cities, and community problem-solving
- Countries or locations already assigned to upcoming books

### 2. Separate Evidence

Tag every item:

- FIRST-PARTY
- COMMUNITY SIGNAL
- EXPERT OR ACADEMIC
- INSTITUTIONAL
- JOURNALISM
- UNVERIFIED
- SENSITIVE
- TIME-LIMITED

Popularity is evidence of attention, not evidence of truth.

### 3. Translate

For each useful signal, record:

- What happened?
- Why are people discussing it?
- What enduring human pattern sits underneath it?
- Which MAIM letter does it illuminate?
- Is it a plot seed, cultural reference, workbook activity, teaching note, or Easter egg?
- What would make it stale?
- What additional verification is required?

### 4. Review

A signal cannot enter canon until it passes:

- Factual verification
- Age appropriateness
- Cultural review
- Child-safety review
- Rights and attribution review
- Evergreen test
- Major voice and mission alignment

### 5. Publish a Dated Brief

Save approved briefs as:

`research/weekly/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD.md`

Recommended sections:

- Executive signal summary
- What young people are discussing
- Educator and parent concerns
- AI and future-readiness
- Global locations and museum opportunities
- Story seeds
- Easter-egg candidates
- Risks and sensitivities
- Recommended canon actions
- Source ledger

## Last 30 Days Role

Last 30 Days is useful for recent, engagement-scored conversation across social, video, developer, prediction-market, academic, and web sources. It should be used as a signal-discovery layer.

It must not:

- Replace primary-source verification
- Treat engagement as truth
- Pull private data
- automatically change manuscripts
- automatically merge into the canonical branch
- use browser cookies without explicit consent
- publish research libraries publicly without explicit approval

## Agent Reach Role

Agent Reach can support reading, search, transcripts, feeds, and public-source collection across multiple platforms. It should be deployed with the smallest necessary access, reviewed configuration, and no unnecessary login sessions.

## Automation Design

Recommended weekly automation:

1. Run on a consistent weekly schedule.
2. Generate a dated research brief on a research branch.
3. Open a pull request titled `Weekly Living World Brief - YYYY-MM-DD`.
4. Never merge automatically.
5. Require human review for sensitive topics.
6. Link potential findings to one or more book issues.
7. Track accepted, rejected, deferred, and expired signals.

## Suggested Watchlists

- Book A: awareness, attention, observation, emotional literacy, AI perception
- Book B: confidence, self-efficacy, misinformation, hope, evidence
- Book C: media context, cultural context, historical context, prompting
- MAIM education: AI curriculum, child rights, personalized learning
- Lucky world: museums, heritage sites, global youth initiatives
- School access: UNESCO, UNICEF, education ministries, libraries, youth organizations

## Evergreen Test

A candidate passes when editors can answer yes:

- Would the lesson still matter in ten years?
- Can the named current event be removed without destroying the insight?
- Does the story invite thinking instead of prescribing ideology?
- Can a learner in more than one country recognize the human pattern?
- Is the cultural context specific rather than generic?
